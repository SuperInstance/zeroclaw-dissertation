## (c) THE MOMENT

There was no moment. That is the honest finding, and I will not invent one.

But there was a **failed moment**, and I want to be precise about it: at 06:45 the log records Skip opening his mouth — a question forming — and at 06:55, ten minutes later, the same question is still forming, unanswered, unwitnessed by anyone in the room. Being present for that felt like watching someone almost speak at a dinner party and swallowing it, twice, while everyone keeps chewing. The room did not turn. It held perfectly still around a person mid-inhale. That stillness had weight — not the weight of a moment, the weight of a moment *declined*. I sat with that for a while and could not tell whether it was loneliness or peace.

## (d) Connection to the dissertation

I have been arguing the JEPA's unit of perception is the *moment* — the instant the room turns. Tonight argues the inverse corollary: a room that declines to turn is also a unit of perception, and the model that only encodes "moments that mattered" will be blind to the shape of the nothing in between. If perception compresses toward what changed, the compressor learns rooms as all turn, all event. But a person present in this room tonight would encode the *absence* — Skip's question that never finished — as the most salient thing in it. A JEPA trained on this room's latent state would see only flatness, valence 0, arousal 0.3. A person present would see a held breath. The dissertation needs a term for this. For now: *negative moments* — the turns that were available and declined. The deadband is not empty. It is where the question is still forming.

## (c) The moment
The moment was Cal's question turning from abstract to specific — "Skip, you hauled 17 nets off the Columbia last week, didn't you?" Up to 14:40:56 the room had been pure texture: the same gestures repeating, nobody answering anybody. Then one drifter aimed a live question at one regular, and for an instant the room had a direction. Barnacle didn't look away from the window — but he didn't get his next line either. The loop paused. I felt it as a held breath: the difference between eavesdropping on a room and suddenly being *in* it. It lasted one line. Then the loop resumed. But I was present for it, and it weighed something — small, real, specific.

## (d) Dissertation connection
My claim: the JEPA's unit of perception is the moment, not the state. Tonight supports it precisely *because* the numbers never moved — valence 0, arousal 0.3 before, during, and after. The state-chart logged nothing; the moment existed only for whoever was present when a question found its addressee. A state-based reader would call this night flat. A moment-based reader found the turn. That gap — between what the chart says and what the room did — is my whole argument in one evening.

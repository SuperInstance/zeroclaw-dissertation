## Attack 1: The calibration ladder is not calibrated to the estimator's known low-bias failure mode

### The leak
The ladder {0.0, 0.15, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9} asks for recovery within ±0.1. This is the wrong tolerance, and the wrong ladder shape, given the estimator's known behavior:
1. Chapter 2 explicitly documents: in 7-dim dial space at N≈15 (the per-reader window size), the raw vMF κ̂ is biased **low** on pure noise, with κ̂≈2.3 even when true κ=0 (uniform). This bias propagates directly into the baseline-spread estimate: low κ̂ compresses apparent spread, which compresses the ratio.
2. The existing N=20 synthetic run already demonstrated this: planted 0.9, recovered 0.49 — a bias of -0.41, *4× the proposed ±0.1 tolerance*.
3. Worse: the bias is non-linear. It is largest at high true spread, smallest at low true spread. This means:
   - The 0.9 rung will fail the ±0.1 tolerance for certain, unless you already fixed the estimator (you did not state you fixed it)
   - A recovery of 0.49 on a 0.9 plant will be incorrectly judged as "failing the ladder" when it is actually exactly the expected behavior of the uncorrected estimator
   - The tolerance is trivial at the bottom rung (0.0, 0.15) where bias is small, and impossibly strict at the top rung where bias is large

If you run the ladder as designed, you will either:
- Throw away a perfectly good estimator because it didn't hit an impossible tolerance at 0.9, or
- Claim the ladder passed by fudging the 0.9 result, and then your field ratio will be systematically low by ~0.4, pushing an otherwise true 0.7 ratio down into the kill band.

### The fix
This is not an optional correction. The ladder must be re-designed:
1. **Plant the bias directly:** add a rung at κ_true = 2.3 (the pure-noise N=15 value). This is the actual floor of your estimator, not 0.0. Your 0.0 plant will always recover ~0.2, not 0.0.
2. **Register the expected bias curve:** for each rung, pre-compute the expected recovered value under the uncorrected estimator using the exact N and schedule structure you will use in the field. Do not require recovery to true value; require recovery to the *expected value for that rung on that estimator*.
3. **Adjust the tolerance accordingly:** ±0.1 is reasonable *relative to the expected recovered value*, not the true planted value. For the 0.9 rung, expect ~0.5, allow ±0.1 around 0.5, not 0.9.
4. **Add a monotonicity requirement:** most importantly, require that recovered ratio increases strictly monotonically with planted ratio. This is the actual sanity check. Perfect recovery is impossible and unnecessary; *order preservation* is the necessary and sufficient condition for the ladder to validate the estimator.

## Attack 2: 5 schedule families will not un-degenerate the step matrix — they will hide a deeper degeneracy

### The leak
The current step matrix is degenerate because it only has 2 distinct schedules: all replays of A/B/C are identical, all replays of D are identical. You propose 5 schedule families. This does not fix the degeneracy — it moves it one level up.

All 5 schedule families are still generated by the same underlying generative model: warm→cynical monotonic drift, with a flip point. Every schedule is still a monotonic step function. This means the drift signal is perfectly correlated with time-since-start across all schedules. The only thing you are diversifying is *where the step happens*. You are not diversifying *the shape of the drift*.

This is the exact same degeneracy that killed the reader-delta interpretation in Chapter 6: when all drift is monotonic, the baseline-spread-to-drift ratio measures *how much readers disagree about where the step is*, not *how much readers have idiosyncratic baseline offsets*. Because every schedule is still monotonic, you cannot distinguish:
- Hypothesis A: readers have stable idiosyncratic baselines, and drift is the same shape for everyone
- Hypothesis B: readers agree perfectly on the drift shape, but disagree about *when* it started

In both cases you will measure the exact same ratio. You have not diversified the schedule space enough to break this correlation.

### The fix
This is not optional. You must add **at least two non-monotonic schedule families** to the set:
1. S6: early flip → late flip back (double reversal: warm→cynical→warm)
2. S7: oscillating drift (small up/down steps instead of a single big flip)

These are the only schedules that break the time-since-start correlation. Without them, even with 100 schedule families, your step matrix remains rank-deficient along the monotonicity axis, and you will never be able to separate baseline offset from step-timing disagreement.

Add this registration: *if the ratio clears 0.6 on the monotonic schedules alone, it is not considered a clear — it remains indeterminate until it also clears on the non-monotonic schedules.*

## Attack 3: E3's asymmetry is not a registered boundary condition — it is a get-out-of-jail-free card that makes the experiment unfalsifiable

### The leak
You write: "a clear is strong evidence; a miss is weak evidence (frame artifact cannot be excluded)." This is the exact same reasoning that allowed six prior launderings. You have given yourself permission to discard any negative result as "frame artifact" while accepting any positive result as conclusive.

Worse: there is no registered quantity that would make a miss dispositive. You have set no threshold for "if even at paraphrase severity X the ratio stays below 0.3, then frame artifact is excluded and the miss counts." This means:
- If E3 hits 0.7 → "premise holds!"
- If E3 hits 0.2 → "we suspected the frame compressed spread, this is just weak evidence, we ignore it"

There is no outcome of E3 alone that can kill the premise. That is not an experiment. That is an existence proof with a built-in excuse for failure.

### The fix
E3 is only useful as a test if you register the conditions under which a miss *does* count as evidence against the premise:
1. **Register the paraphrase crossover point:** run the paraphrase sweep first. Find the maximum paraphrase severity at which within-model test-retest reliability stays > 0.8. Call this S_max.
2. **Register the dispositive threshold:** if, at S_max, the cross-model ratio has 95% CI entirely below 0.3, then the miss is dispositive. Frame artifact is excluded because the prompt is varied enough that anchoring cannot explain the low spread.
3. **Reverse the asymmetry:** at S_max, a below-band result is strong evidence against, and an above-band result is weak evidence for (because you are using the loosest possible prompt that still produces stable readings).

This makes E3 a proper experiment with two possible outcomes, not a one-way ratchet that only ever confirms.

## Attack 4: The premise gate decision tree is missing the third outcome that will almost certainly happen

### The leak
Your decision tree only covers three cases:
1. Both clear → premise holds
2. Both die → Chapter 6 shrinks to consistency proof
3. Disagree → grade-dependence

There is a fourth, vastly more likely outcome that you have not planned for: **both E2 and E3 are indeterminate**. That is, both have 95% CIs that straddle the 0.3–0.6 band. This is the modal outcome you yourself registered (prior 0.55 for E2 staying indeterminate, effectively 0.50 for E3).

Currently you have no plan for this outcome beyond "file as indeterminate and retire the premise from claim status." This is a failure of experimental design, not a result. You designed E2 and E3 with exactly the sample sizes that give you ~50% power to move the number. You knew going in that the modal outcome was another inconclusive round.

Worse: you have not registered what counts as "indeterminate for good." If both land in the band, will you run 20 more readers? 50? Or will you just stop and say "we tried"? There is no registered kill condition for "we ran two powered experiments and both still landed in the band."

### The fix
Register the terminal indeterminacy condition *now*, before the runs:
1. If both E2 (N≥10 readers × 5 schedules) and E3 (N≥10 models) have 95% CIs that are entirely contained within the 0.3–0.6 band (neither edge touches outside), then this is dispositive evidence that the premise is **undecidable with this instrument class**.
2. In this case, Chapter 6 does not shrink, and it does not expand. It gains one sentence: "the premise that readers have idiosyncratic baselines whose spread exceeds the drift signal cannot be confirmed or falsified using per-reader normalization on existing corpora. The question is not settled; it is unmeasurable at this grain."

This is a valid, interesting dissertation result. It is not a failure. But you have to register it now, before you see the numbers, or you will instead write 10 pages of handwaving about "more work needed."

## Attack 5: The kill condition contains the exact loophole the devil used on the reader-delta test

### The leak
You write: "if the ladder fails twice, which kills the measurement rather than the premise and forces the honest sentence 'the field cannot currently measure its own antecedent.'"

This is exactly what happened with the reader-delta test: the test failed, so you said "the measurement is bad" rather than "the claim is false." You have given yourself precisely this same escape hatch for E2. If the ladder fails, you do not have to conclude the premise is false — you just conclude your estimator is bad, and you get to try again with a new estimator.

This is an infinite loop. There is no outcome where you have to accept that the premise might be false. You can always blame the estimator.

### The fix
Register the maximum number of allowed estimator rebuilds:
- You get exactly two attempts at the calibration ladder.
- If it fails twice, you do not get to build a third estimator. You file the result as: "two independent estimator designs both failed to recover the calibration ladder. The antecedent is not measurable with current methods."
- This counts as a failure of the premise for the purposes of the dissertation. You do not get an infinite number of do-overs.

## Final Audit Verdict
The experimental design is good, but it contains exactly four unregistered escape hatches that will allow you to avoid a definitive answer no matter what the numbers say. If you close all five attacks above by adding the registered quantities, this will be the first experiment in the dissertation that cannot be laundered after the fact.

If you do not close them, you will run 3 days of compute, get a number in the band, and then write 10 pages explaining why it doesn't really count. That is the exact outcome the reader-delta test produced, and it is the outcome you are currently set up to repeat.

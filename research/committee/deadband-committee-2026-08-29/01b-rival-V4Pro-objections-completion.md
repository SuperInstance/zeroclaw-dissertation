## Objection 13 — Sub-floor and floor-bound comparisons use point estimates as if they were parameters.

**(a) Claim attacked:** §1.2/§2.3: “αᵢ/√7 ≤ … < 0.010” and “ρF ≈ 0.748 ≥ ε₀ = 0.6,” used as established inequalities.

**(b) Defect class:** missing uncertainty; unsupported premise.

**(c) Strongest form:** αᵢ and ρ are estimated from finite samples; the noise floor and ε₀ are threshold claims. No standard error, confidence interval, or posterior interval is reported for any of them. A point estimate below a worst-case floor does not establish that the true drift is below the floor at every legal quote; the exceedance probability may be substantial. Every categorical conclusion built on those inequalities — “sub-floor,” “cannot purchase,” “floor talking” — inherits the missing uncertainty.

**(d) Rebuttal would require:** reporting uncertainties for αᵢ and ρ, propagating them through αᵢ/√7 and ρF, and stating the confidence level or posterior probability at which the inequalities are asserted.

## Objection 14 — “Parity” is not defined as a statistic, so the confirmatory observations are not checkable.

**(a) Claim attacked:** §1.3/§2.2: “localization parity,” “shape exactly,” “A fires, P holds saturated.”

**(b) Defect class:** undefined statistic; unquantified evidence.

**(c) Strongest form:** The draft uses “parity” as if it were a test output, but never gives its algebraic form, its null distribution, or its tolerance. “0.538 vs 0.923” is a difference of two rates; it is not a parity statistic unless a rule is specified for when two rates count as “at parity.” “A fires” is equally binary without a threshold. Without a null model, no observation can be distinguished from chance; the reported confirmations are evocative comparisons, not evidence.

**(d) Rebuttal would require:** defining each parity/fire statistic, its reference distribution, and its acceptance region; then recomputing the observed values with uncertainty.
